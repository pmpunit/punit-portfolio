Start with the destination

As Senior Product Manager, I owned the end-to-end Incident Management experience in Jira Service Management and shaped the strategy toward automated incident resolution.

The path progressed from playbooks to runbooks and, ultimately, intelligent resolution. Each step depended on a foundation: knowledge of what had worked in previous incidents.

01Playbooks

Guide the response.

02Runbooks

Make resolution steps repeatable.

03Intelligent resolution

Use incident knowledge to inform action.

Strategic progression toward automated resolution; not a claim that every stage was delivered.

Find the missing foundation

Major-incident closure and post-incident report creation stood at 0.2%. Capturing the lesson still required more work after the immediate disruption was over.

The opportunity was to make that step easier. A report users could review and approve would reduce the effort of documenting an incident and help preserve the context needed for future resolution.

Make the first draft useful

Built automated PIR creation using generative AI and incident context. Instead of starting with a blank report, users received a contextual draft and retained control through review and approval.

01Incident context

Bring the relevant information together.

02Generated draft

Create the post-incident account.

03Human approval

Review the record before accepting it.

I owned the work end to end, alongside the broader incident management journey. AI served a specific purpose: make valuable knowledge easier to capture.

Earn trust before expanding

Atlassian was customer zero. Shadow testing compared generated summaries with customer-written reports, followed by customer testing and phased releases.

Each phase was gated by quality checks, with an accuracy threshold above 80% as the release criterion. Testing and evaluation shaped the rollout before broader availability.

Major-incident closure and PIR creation rate

The rate reached 14% in one quarter and 34% over three quarters. Making reports easier to create helped establish the incident knowledge needed for the longer-term ambition.

Across the wider incident management experience, monthly active users grew from 60,000 to 340,000 and CSAT rose from 43 to 72. Those outcomes reflect the broader product work, beyond PIR automation alone.
