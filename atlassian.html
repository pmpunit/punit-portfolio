The service is down. Coordinating the response is still manual.

The ticket is in JSM, but bringing responders together, collaborating and keeping stakeholders informed happens elsewhere. Teams must coordinate that work while trying to restore service.

A ticket did not resolve the incident

Customers treated Jira Service Management as a ticketing system. The incident was recorded there, but much of the actual response happened outside it: finding responders, coordinating their work and keeping stakeholders updated.

Users wanted incidents resolved with minimum manual effort. The opportunity was to bring the response into JSM and make the product useful throughout resolution.

Bring the response together

As Senior Product Manager, I shaped the strategy and built the end-to-end Incident Management experience. Advanced capabilities made JSM useful during the incident, not just a place to record it.

Mobilise responders

Add responders and alert them to bring the right people into the response.

Coordinate the work

Build the Incident Command Center, integrating Slack and video conferencing for collaboration.

Keep stakeholders informed

Provide real-time updates while the incident is being handled.

Capture what worked

Introduce post-incident reports to preserve how the incident was resolved.

Across the broader incident management experience, monthly active users grew from 60,000 to 340,000 and CSAT improved from 43 to 72.

Make the next step possible

The long-term strategy was automated incident resolution: a path from playbooks to runbooks and intelligent resolution. Learning from past incidents required knowledge of how they had been resolved.

But writing a post-incident report still meant more manual work. Major-incident closure and report creation stood at 0.2%. Introducing PIRs was a foundation; making them easier to complete was the next step.

Let AI do the drafting

I built AI-generated post-incident reports from incident context. Users received a draft to review and approve, rather than having to write the account from scratch.

Atlassian was customer zero. Shadow testing compared generated summaries with customer-written reports, followed by customer testing and phased releases. Each phase had quality gates, with an accuracy threshold above 80% as a release criterion.
