How I work

Three things I'll bring on day one.

Make the ambiguous tractable

Most hard product problems aren't hard to build — they're hard to state. Research, market analysis and internal alignment exist to get a vague problem into a shape a team can actually attack.

Measure what compounds

Stickiness, conversion and customer lifetime value, not shipped-feature counts. Prioritisation follows from what those numbers say, which is what makes the trade-offs arguable in the open.

Stay close to the engineering

A CS degree and a decade next to distributed systems means I can hold a real conversation about the constraint, not just relay it. Fewer translation losses, faster decisions.

About

A bit more.

I started out evaluating business cases at Daimler, which sounds like an odd beginning for a product career until you notice it's the same job: decide what's worth building, and be able to defend the number.

Since then I've mostly worked where the data is dense and the user is impatient — developers debugging at 2am, IT teams triaging an outage, analysts waiting on a pipeline. The common problem in all of it is that the system knows what's wrong and has no good way of saying so. That's the gap I keep coming back to.

I'm a computer science graduate from VIT and did my PGDM at IIM Shillong. Somewhere in between I cleared CFA Level I, which is mostly useful now for reading a P&L without flinching.
